# Conference submission timeline — shared reference

*SUBMISSION deadlines (abstract / full paper only) for the venues this repo's papers target. Not paper-specific — consult it for ANY publication planning. **Structure (fixed): exactly TWO lists — §Conferences and §Workshops — each in strict time order, one row per time point, abstract and full paper always separate rows.** Keep review status, paper→venue mapping, and strategy reasoning OUT of this file (public repo; that lives in the gitignored TODO.md / psyche portfolio). Maintenance: when a CFP is announced or a date shifts, update the row and the "verified" date; entries marked EXPECTED are extrapolated from the previous year and must be re-verified before planning against them.*

*Deadlines last verified: 2026-07-16 · Archival column verified: 2026-07-20. All deadlines AoE (UTC-12) unless noted.*

*Scoring columns (judged 2026-07-16; revisit when research direction shifts). **Rep (0–10)** = the venue's OWN reputation/prestige, paper-agnostic — the dominant consideration when choosing where to submit. For workshops it combines the parent-conference brand, the series' maturity/track record, and the fact that workshop papers are typically NON-archival (rep ceiling well below main conferences: even the strongest workshop series sits ~6). Anchors: 10 = NeurIPS-class main track · 9 = AAAI/ICLR class · 7–8 = strong established conference · 6–6.5 = respected young/niche conference or the top workshop series · 4 = typical first-edition NeurIPS workshop · ≤3.5 = narrow-pool or non-research venue. **Fit (0–10)** = topical fit to this repo's research lines (jailbreak attack/defense evaluation, LLM-judge reliability, VLM safety; quantum-ML side line) — secondary to Rep, tie-breaks among comparable venues and gates what is submittable at all (Fit ≤2 = would not submit regardless of Rep). **Bar (0–10)** = acceptance difficulty on ONE unified scale across both tables: 9 ≈ top conference main track (~20% accept, strongest pool) · 7 ≈ strong conference / Findings-backed cycle · 5–6 ≈ competitive established workshop series · 3–4 ≈ typical first-edition workshop · ≤2 ≈ accepts most reasonable on-topic submissions. First-edition workshop bars have no history — treat ±1.*

*The **Archival** column (verified 2026-07-20 from venue CFPs + NeurIPS workshop guidance) records whether accepted papers enter formal published proceedings. Why it matters: an ARCHIVAL acceptance counts as prior publication — the same paper cannot then be submitted to another archival venue, and archival venues bar simultaneous submission to other archival venues (arXiv preprints and non-archival workshop versions are the standard exceptions — AAAI-27 states both explicitly). A NON-ARCHIVAL acceptance leaves the paper free to go to an archival venue later, and usually to several non-archival venues at once — but per-workshop dual-submission policies vary, so re-verify the target CFPs before cross-submitting (specifics in each table's notes).*

### NeurIPS 2026 workshops

*NeurIPS 2026 runs Dec 6–12 in Sydney with satellite venues in Atlanta and Paris (Dec 9–13). Workshop papers: suggested submission date 2026-08-29 AoE, mandatory accept/reject notification 2026-09-29 (hard). 26 workshop venues registered on OpenReview as of 2026-07-16 (acceptance notifications went out 07-11; more may appear — re-check the official neurips.cc workshops page once it's up).*

*Deadlines are from OpenReview submission invitations where configured (the binding source), else the workshop site. ★ = closest topical fit for this repo's research lines. All rows share the NeurIPS parent brand, so Rep here tracks series maturity + pool quality (all non-archival → ceiling ~6). Fit is the real discriminator: for the ⑧ routing decision (TODO item 1), filter by Fit first (what's submittable at all), then rank survivors by Rep. Time order: AoE = UTC-12, so "08-29 AoE" ends 08-30 11:59 UTC — later than any 08-29 UTC time; rows with no stated timezone are placed by calendar date.*

*Archival (verified 2026-07-20): NeurIPS policy makes EVERY NeurIPS workshop non-archival — "All NeurIPS workshop papers are non-archival and therefore do not appear in proceedings" (neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/WorkshopsGuidance) — so the column below only distinguishes whether the workshop's own CFP states it ("stated") or is silent / not yet published ("NeurIPS rule"). Multi-submission consequence: one paper CAN generally be submitted to several NeurIPS workshops at once — there is NO central NeurIPS rule against it; it is per-workshop (TCCML explicitly allows it, OPT explicitly discourages it, most CFPs are silent) — re-verify the target CFPs before cross-submitting. Two near-universal per-workshop restrictions: work already PUBLISHED at an archival ML venue is excluded almost everywhere, while work merely UNDER REVIEW (e.g. at NeurIPS main) is welcome almost everywhere.*
